# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and maintainers pledge to make participation in DEDSEC a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, professional, and healthy technical community.

## Our standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment include:

- demonstrating empathy, patience, and respect toward other people;
- respecting differing technical opinions, backgrounds, and levels of experience;
- giving specific, constructive code-review feedback focused on the work rather than the person;
- accepting responsibility for mistakes, correcting them, and learning from the experience;
- crediting contributors and security researchers fairly;
- protecting private information shared during coordinated security disclosure;
- focusing on the long-term quality and safety of the project.

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- sexualized language or imagery, or sexual attention or advances of any kind;
- trolling, insults, derogatory comments, or personal and political attacks directed at participants;
- public or private harassment, intimidation, threats, or sustained disruption;
- publishing another person's private information without explicit permission;
- doxxing researchers, maintainers, contributors, or users;
- intentionally publishing private vulnerability reports, credentials, scan artifacts, or sensitive target data shared in confidence;
- pressuring contributors to run tests against systems they are not authorized to assess;
- using project spaces to coordinate abusive, destructive, or clearly unauthorized activity;
- other conduct that could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional technical setting.

## Security-research collaboration

DEDSEC is a security project, and technical discussions may involve vulnerabilities, offensive-security concepts, and scanner behavior. That subject matter does not reduce the standards for professional conduct.

When discussing security findings:

- separate criticism of code or methodology from criticism of the person who wrote or reported it;
- do not shame researchers for good-faith reports or honest false positives;
- keep embargoed or privately disclosed information confidential;
- use synthetic or authorized examples when public reproduction details would expose a third party;
- follow [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) for vulnerabilities in DEDSEC itself.

Good-faith technical disagreement—including strong disagreement about severity, exploitability, false positives, architecture, or testing methodology—is welcome when it remains evidence-based and respectful.

## Enforcement responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, pull requests, wiki edits, or other contributions that are inconsistent with this Code of Conduct, and should communicate moderation reasons when appropriate and safe to do so.

Maintainers must handle conduct reports with appropriate discretion, protect reporter privacy, and avoid unnecessary disclosure of sensitive security or personal information.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all DEDSEC community spaces, including repository issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, project documentation, and other official collaboration channels. It also applies when someone is officially representing the project in public spaces.

## Reporting conduct concerns

Report abusive, harassing, threatening, or otherwise unacceptable behavior **privately** to the repository maintainer using a private contact method listed on the maintainer's GitHub profile.

Do not put sensitive conduct reports, personal information, or private security material into a public issue.

If no appropriate private maintainer contact is available, or if the concern involves the maintainer, use GitHub's platform reporting and abuse-reporting mechanisms as appropriate.

All reports should be reviewed promptly, fairly, and with respect for the privacy and safety of everyone involved. Retaliation against someone for making a good-faith report is unacceptable.

## Enforcement guidelines

Maintainers will consider context, severity, impact, prior behavior, and willingness to correct the conduct when deciding on consequences.

### 1. Correction

**Community impact:** Inappropriate language or behavior that is unprofessional or unwelcome but limited in scope.

**Consequence:** A private written correction explaining the issue and the expected standard. An apology or edit may be requested.

### 2. Warning

**Community impact:** A significant incident or repeated behavior after a correction.

**Consequence:** A formal warning with conditions on continued participation. Contact with affected participants may be restricted for a defined period.

### 3. Temporary restriction

**Community impact:** Serious misconduct, sustained disruption, harassment, or disregard for previous warnings.

**Consequence:** Temporary restriction from project interaction or contribution for a defined period.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Community impact:** A pattern of serious violations, targeted harassment, threats, doxxing, deliberate exposure of private security information, or other behavior incompatible with safe project participation.

**Consequence:** Permanent removal from project community spaces to the extent available to maintainers.
